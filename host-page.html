Publishers Are Quietly Rebuilding the Audio Player, and Readers Have Opinions

Embedded players have become a standard fixture of the article page. What they should look like once they get there is still an open question.

By A Staff Writer · Plus additional reporting · 8 min read

Placeholder artwork. No licensed photography is used on this page.

The audio player sitting in the right rail of an article is one of the least examined pieces of a modern publishing page. It arrives as an iframe, it is handed a fixed width and a fixed height by a template nobody has revisited in years, and it is expected to make sense to a reader who came for the article and was not thinking about listening to anything at all.

That constraint is the interesting part. A player designed in isolation can be any size it likes. A player designed for this slot gets 352 pixels across on a wide desktop, rather less on a tablet, and a hard 300 pixels of height regardless. Every decision about artwork, metadata and transport controls has to survive that budget.

The slot does not adapt to the player. The player adapts to the slot, or it gets clipped.

The rail narrows before it disappears. At a tablet width the same three column span resolves to roughly 221 pixels, which is narrower than most players are ever tested at, and below the tablet breakpoint the rail stops being a rail at all and stacks underneath the article at close to full width.

What that means in practice is that a design has to be legible at three quite different widths without a redraw, and has to do it while sitting on whatever artwork the show happens to have uploaded. Some of that artwork is dark, some is nearly white, and a good deal of it has text baked into it already.

The players in the selector at the top of this page are the same code running in the same iframe, swapped in place. The surrounding page does not change between them, which is the only way to see which one actually holds up in the space it is given.

Scroll position matters too. The slot sits well down the rail, below the first advertisement and the most read list, which means a reader reaches it having already committed to the article. Whether a player should announce itself at that point or stay quiet until asked is exactly the sort of question the designs disagree about.

None of this is settled. It is, however, measurable, and measuring it in the slot rather than on a blank canvas is the cheapest way to avoid shipping a player that only works in the file it was drawn in.

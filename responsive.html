The model

The card responds to the box it is given, not to the viewport. Everything here is driven by CSS container queries on the element that wraps the player, so a 300px card in a narrow sidebar behaves exactly like a 300px card on a 4K monitor. There are no @media width rules in the layout.

Two container modes, and the difference matters

Wrappercontainer-typeQueries that applyWhen
.shellinline-size Width only Default. The host gives a width; the card keeps its own default height.
.shell[data-fill]size Width and height The host gives an explicit height, via --player-h. The card fills it.

The single most important thing to know. Every height rule on this page is inert unless the wrapper carries data-fill. A size container needs a definite height to resolve against, so without one the height queries never match and the card simply sits at its default. In the prototype the height control sets this; in an embed, embed.html sets it so the iframe fills whatever the publisher's slot gives it.

    Rules in effect

      Height

      Four thresholds, each one set by measuring where the layout actually breaks rather than by picking a round number. Below each, something gives way so that the play button is never cut and never drops under its 40px minimum.

      HeightWhat changesWhy there
      300 and up The episode / featured-artist list becomes inline under the player. The list button disappears from the top bar and the drawer is disabled. The shortest card that can hold a usable list, at its 128px minimum, plus a stage that still clears its own floor.
      299 down to 128 The full stacked hero. Play button centred over the artwork, metadata and actions in the top bar, lockup and scrubber at the foot. The list returns as a drawer. The reference layout. Both published defaults, 300 and 200, sit here.
      127 down to 76 Compact row. The play button leaves the centre and takes the left of the metadata row, beside the artwork tile. Info, list and share are hidden, as are seek, speed and skip. The lockup stays below. Measured: a centred button and the top bar collide at 128, because they compete for the same vertical space. Moving the button into that row removes the competition instead of resolving it, so the metadata survives instead of being hidden.
      75 down to 56 The bottom block goes: scrubber and the Listen on iHeart lockup. The card is the row and the artwork behind it. Measured: at 76 the block still clears the row by 3px; at 70 it is already past the bottom of the card.
      55 down to 50 The 8px card inset gives way, reaching 5px at 50. A 40px button with 8px above and below needs 56. Under that, the inset is the only variable left that keeps the button whole and at its stated minimum.

      The play button through the range

      It holds 64 for as long as the geometry allows and only then shrinks, rather than scaling on a ratio from the top. Measured, podcast card at 411 wide.

      Card height900300200 176160150136 12910050
      Play button646464 64645441 404040

      Why two formulas. The button is centred on the card, so each side has to clear its own bar and each bound is twice the room on that side. A card with a scrubber has a taller bottom block, so the bottom binds; live radio, artist radio and playlist have no scrubber, so the top bar binds instead. Both bounds are the height minus a constant, so which one wins never depends on the height, and each rule carries only the bound that can actually bind.

      Width

      Far less happens across width than across height. The card is fluid, the metadata truncates and marquees on its own, and only two things actually switch.

      WidthWhat changesWhy
      319 and under The pause overlay's copy and its call to action step down a size. The overlay is centred text over the artwork; at this width the larger size wraps badly. Off by default in the prototype.
      239 and under The words Listen on are hidden. The iHeart logotype stays. The lockup is the last thing on its row and the words are the part that can go without losing the attribution.
      Very narrow The play button is bound by the card's width as well as its height, through 100cqw - 8px. At a 50px-wide card it measures 42. A narrow card can run out of room across before it runs out down.

      Not a breakpoint, but width-driven. Every metadata line is measured after layout and gets a marquee only when its text really is wider than its box, which depends on the card's current width. So the same title scrolls at 240 and sits still at 500 with no rule involved.

      Continuous values

      These interpolate rather than switching. cqh and cqw are percentages of the container's height and width. Every one is wrapped in round(…, 1px), because the clamps otherwise land on fractions and a card whose rows all start on a subpixel boundary renders visibly soft.

      Implementation notes

      Things that will bite you

      NoteDetail
      The live card overrides its own controls .widget.c.live .hero-controls sets its own top to nudge the button half a pixel. That is more specific than a plain .widget.c and later in the file, so any rule repositioning the controls has to carry both selectors or it will move every card except live.
      The artwork tile is sized in JavaScript It is square and derived from the height of the text block beside it. Three CSS-only attempts failed: the image stretched to 3000px through a circular dependency, and a link with a min-height collapsed to 6px wide inside a size container. It is measured after layout instead.
      The play button animates its own size .hero-play carries transition: 300ms, an all-properties shorthand rather than a transform-only one, so width and height animate after any resize. Measure it after the transition settles or you will read a size the card has already moved on from.
      Transport buttons only exist once playing Seek, speed and skip are hidden until the card has started, so measuring them on a freshly loaded card returns zero.
      A size container needs a definite height container-type: size with no resolvable height makes every height query fail silently. This is why --player-h and data-fill are set together and never one without the other.
      There is no floor any more The card used to stop at a minimum height and let the slot clip the overflow. Because the button is centred, that cut it in half. The card is now exactly its slot down to 50 and the chrome gives way instead.

      Where the rules live

      FileWhat is in it
      widget-core.css Every container query and every clamp. Search for @container.
      widget-core.js The tile measurement, the marquee measurement and the drawn scrollbar, all of which react to size but cannot be expressed in CSS.
      embed.html Sets data-fill so an embedded card fills the publisher's slot, and reports its preferred height back to the host.
      test-play-visible.py Sweeps all five content types across fifteen heights either side of each threshold and asserts the button is never cut, never under 40, and never overlapped.
      test-height.py The inline list threshold, the fill behaviour and the height control itself.
